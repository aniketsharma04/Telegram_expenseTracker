# Aaj wins as India's neutral money hub

Aaj can become a credible "all your finances in one place" app for Indian users without a licence or a budget, but only by building on documents and messages the user brings in, never on regulated data pipes it can't legally use. The official rails are closed to an unregistered solo developer. Account Aggregator data goes only to RBI-, SEBI-, IRDAI- or PFRDA-regulated entities. Bureau credit scores and BBPS bill fetch need a registered business and paid contracts. Production Gmail access needs a paid annual security assessment. What stays free and legal: on-device SMS and notification parsing, Gmail alerts forwarded to an Aaj inbound address, CAS statement parsing for mutual funds and demat holdings, EPF/NPS/PPF statement uploads, user-entered credit scores, and `upi://pay` hand-offs for bills. The market gap is real. Every large Indian tracker now earns its money from loans, cards or investments: axio is part of Amazon, Moneyview is a loan funnel, and INDmoney and CRED push their own products. Fi has wound down its banking. Google Pay's new free Gemini assistant covers only Google Pay's own history. That leaves room for a neutral, chat-first, family-aware tracker that covers cash and every bank. Five parts of the current stack carry legal or data-loss risk and should be fixed before any new feature ships: bank text sent to Gemini's free tier, Gmail's 100-user lifetime cap, Vercel Hobby's ban on commercial use, the lack of backups on Supabase Free, and the mocked BBPS fetch. The roadmap below sequences that cleanup first, then retention features backed by evidence (weekly personalised nudges, subscription detection, tax and festival planning), then investments and net worth, and leaves anything that needs money, an entity or a licence for last, each with a free fallback.

## Every incumbent sells loans or investments, leaving the neutral tracker open

Indian personal finance apps have settled into two models, and both treat tracking as bait. The first is the SMS-parsing tracker attached to a lender. Amazon closed its roughly **$200M acquisition of axio** (formerly Capital Float, and the home of the old Walnut tracker) in September 2025 after RBI approval ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/industry/news/amazon-completes-acquisition-axio-rbi-approval-125090401170_1.html)). The axio app still parses transactional SMS, and it now sits next to Pay Later EMIs, personal loans and fixed deposits ([APKMirror](https://www.apkmirror.com/apk/axio-digital-pvt-ltd/axio-expense-tracker-bnpl/axio-expense-tracker-budget-8-9-6-release/)). Moneyview runs the same playbook, and most public complaints about it concern its loans, not its tracker: auto-debit errors, double debits, approved loans that never arrived ([Voxya](https://voxya.com/consumer-complaints/money-view-auto-debit-double-payment-in-month/219257)). The second model is Account Aggregator consolidation attached to an investing or card business. INDmoney's net worth view is free and pulls from depositories and AAs, but a family view works only if **every member holds their own INDmoney account** ([INDmoney](https://www.indmoney.com/networth/family-accounts)). CRED Money, launched in July 2024, consolidates balances across banks and UPI IDs for CRED's affluent card base ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/24/cred-launches-personal-finance-manager-for-indias-affluent/)).

Two 2026 events change the picture. Fi, after layoffs, told customers in March 2026 that banking services "will soon be discontinued" and that it is pivoting to enterprise AI ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/11/india-neobank-fi-winds-down-banking-services-on-its-platform)). That is the same "the app I trusted died" story Mint users went through in the US in 2024 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/07/budgeting-app-mint-is-shutting-down-users-are-disappointed.html)). Then, in August 2026, Google Pay launched **"Ask Google Pay"**, a free Gemini assistant. It analyses a user's Google Pay history by text or voice in **10 Indian languages** ([Tech2](https://www.tech2.com/apps/google-pay-gets-a-gemini-powered-chatbot-for-spending-insights-in-india-text-10240689.html)). So Hindi chat and voice alone no longer set Aaj apart. What Google Pay cannot do is see other UPI apps, cash, cards, Gmail bills or a parent's spending.

Aaj therefore has to win on four things together, and no competitor offers all four. The first is coverage across sources: SMS, notifications, email, chat and voice for cash, plus statements. The second is neutrality: "we never sell you a loan" directly contrasts Aaj with axio, Moneyview, CRED and INDmoney. The third is family support without forced sign-ups. The fourth is a coaching layer: Aaj's 40% EMI cap and its car and home affordability rules are not advertised by any incumbent. Privacy is the price of entry rather than an edge, because a wave of 2025–26 indie apps already markets "no SMS permission" and on-device parsing ([Finny](https://getfinny.app/blog/sms-expense-tracking-app)). Global pricing points the same way. Monarch costs about $99.99 a year and YNAB about $109 ([Costbench](https://www.costbench.com/software/personal-finance/monarch-money/)), but no Indian incumbent charges for tracking alone. Aaj needs a generous free core.

## User-supplied documents beat licensed data pipes for a solo developer

The data-access question splits cleanly. The rails that would make Aaj a true consolidator are legally closed today. The user-initiated workarounds cover almost the same ground for free.

Account Aggregator is the clearest example. Sahamati states that a Financial Information User "must be registered and regulated by at least one Financial Service Regulator (RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, PFRDA)" ([Sahamati](https://sahamati.org.in/?p=1224)). Technical service providers such as Setu or Finvu only run the FIU module _for_ a regulated entity; they cannot be the licensed party ([ecorpit](https://ecorpit.com/account-aggregator-integration-fintech-builders-2026/)). From **1 November 2026**, FIUs pay a standardised fee, and participants cannot charge customers for sharing data ([CAalley](https://caalley.com/news-updates/indian-news/explained-what-is-account-aggregator-framework-and-how-does-it-work)). Even through a partner, a free app would absorb every fetch. AA also does not yet carry **EPF, PPF, bonds or G-Secs**; they are proposed only, with no data providers live ([ecorpit](https://ecorpit.com/account-aggregator-integration-fintech-builders-2026/)). Credit bureaus are similar. Aggregator APIs cost about **₹35–55 per pull** and need a business entity and KYC ([Decentro](https://decentro.tech/resources/credit-bureau-api)). Experian, by contrast, gives consumers a free score on WhatsApp ([Experian](https://www.experianplc.com/newsroom/press-releases/2022/experian-becomes-first-credit-bureau-in-india-to-offer-free-credit-scores-on-whatsapp)). BBPS agents need a registered business, KYC documents, and SOP and pricing agreements with an operating unit such as Setu ([Setu BillPay](https://docs.setu.co/payments/billpay/api-integration)). An agent faces no stated net-worth floor ([Axis Bank](https://axisbank.com/business-banking/bharat-bill-payment-system/agent/got-a-query)), but no individual route exists.

The free routes are just as concrete. For mutual funds and demat holdings, users request a Consolidated Account Statement from CAMS or KFintech by PAN and email ([AMFI](https://amfiindia.com/investor-corner/online-center/download-CAS.html)). The MIT-licensed `casparser` library parses CAMS, KFintech, NSDL and CDSL statements into JSON. It does **not** support MF Central statements, so onboarding should point users to CAMS or KFintech ([GitHub](https://github.com/codereverser/casparser)). The EPF passbook downloads as a PDF from the EPFO portal or UMANG ([Groww](https://groww.in/p/savings-schemes/epf-passbook-download)). For bills, NPCI made UPI Intent the mobile standard from 28 February 2026 ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/docs/announcements/upi-collect-migration/recurring-payments/custom-checkout)). Generating a `upi://pay?pa=…&am=…` link needs no licence, because the user's own UPI app makes the payment ([Solidgate](https://docs.solidgate.com/payments/alternative-payments/apms-overview/upi/upi-intent/)). Aaj gets no success callback, so it should confirm payment later from the bank SMS.

Gmail is the access point Aaj already depends on, and the most urgent one. gmail.readonly is a restricted scope. Unverified apps are capped at **100 new users over the project's lifetime**, a cap that "cannot be reset", and in Testing mode refresh tokens **expire after 7 days** ([Google Cloud Help](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945?hl=en)). Going past that needs a CASA Tier 2 assessment every year. TAC Security lists it at **$540–$1,800 per year** ([TAC Security](https://tacsecurity.com/esof/)). The free path is to drop OAuth for most users. The user sets a Gmail filter that forwards bank and biller alerts to an Aaj inbound address. That involves no restricted scope and no assessment, and the user controls what is shared. Cloudflare Email Routing to a Worker is one free way to receive the mail. This is the research team's inference rather than a tested setup. Gmail asks the user to confirm a new forwarding address, so the Worker has to show or relay that confirmation code.

| Data                      | Free, solo, now                                   | Needs entity or money           | Needs licence or regulated partner |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Bank and UPI transactions | On-device SMS and notification parsing            | —                               | AA (FIU)                           |
| Email alerts and bills    | Forward-to-inbound address; OAuth under 100 users | Gmail CASA, $540+/yr            | —                                  |
| Mutual funds and stocks   | CAS PDF plus `casparser`                          | —                               | AA via FIU                         |
| EPF, NPS, PPF             | Statement upload or manual entry                  | —                               | Not on AA yet (EPF, PPF)           |
| Credit score              | User enters it or forwards the report             | Bureau aggregator, ₹35–55/pull  | CIC membership                     |
| Bill fetch and pay        | Parse bill emails and SMS; `upi://pay` hand-off   | BBPS agent via Setu or Cashfree | BBPOU                              |
| Autopay mandates          | Track from SMS and email only                     | Payment aggregator partner      | PSP                                |

## Track and teach freely; recommend, refer or hold money only with a licence

The regulatory boundary is simple to state and easy to cross by accident. **Expense tracking, budgeting rules (50/30/20, EMI caps, emergency fund) and read-only portfolio display are not regulated advice.** Naming a mutual fund or stock for a particular user is. Under SEBI's Investment Adviser Regulations, anyone who "acts as" or "holds itself out as" an adviser must register. The exemption covers only good-faith comments on market trends that name no particular security ([Nishith Desai Associates](https://nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/corpsec-hotline/investment-advisers-time-to-register-8408)). AI gets no carve-out. SEBI's December 2024 amendment makes _registered_ advisers responsible for their AI tools and requires them to disclose AI use ([Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/law-policy/sebis-amendment-to-investment-advisor-regulations-addresses-ai-concerns)). An unregistered app whose chatbot suggests funds is still giving unregistered advice. Personalised asset allocation ("you should hold 60% equity") sits in a grey zone. Treat it as advice, or offer it only as a calculator driven by the user's own inputs, with disclaimers. Affiliate income is a trap as well. SEBI's finfluencer circular bars regulated entities, including MF distributors, from paying or partnering with unregistered people who give advice ([Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/amp/finance/news/sebi-gives-3-month-window-to-end-associations-with-unregistered-entities-124102201169_1.html)).

The RBI and IRDAI lines follow the same logic. Showing balances parsed from the user's own messages is not an RBI-regulated activity. Offering loans makes Aaj a Lending Service Provider under the **Digital Lending Directions, 2025**, with a written agreement with a regulated lender ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/FS_Notification.aspx?Id=12848)), and on Play India it must also appear on RBI's digital-lending-app list ([Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876821)). Holding or settling funds needs payment-aggregator authorisation and **₹15 crore net worth** ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/rbi-issues-new-master-directions-for-payment-aggregators-rules-take-effect-immediately)), so bill pay has to stay a hand-off. Insurance quotes or lead fees need IRDAI web-aggregator, broker or agent registration ([Policyholder.gov.in](https://policyholder.gov.in/web/guest/distribution-development/web-aggregators)). Tracking the user's own policies and sending renewal reminders does not.

Data protection is the regime that applies to everything Aaj already does. The DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025. Core fiduciary duties (notice, security, breach reporting, user rights, children) become enforceable around **13 May 2027** ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/11/digital-personal-data-protection-dpdp-rules-2025-rollout-over-18-months)). Breaches must be reported to the Board without delay, followed by a detailed report **within 72 hours** ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1706890/a-closer-look-at-the-dpdp-rules-2025)). Security failures carry penalties of up to **₹250 crore** ([Konfirmity](https://www.konfirmity.com/blog/dpdp-penalties)). A "child" is anyone under 18 and needs verifiable parental consent ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/12/26/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-key-highlights/)), so an 18+ gate is the simple answer for a finance app. These summaries come from law firms; nobody fetched the Gazette text.

The platform rules arrive sooner than the law. Google Play explicitly allows READ_SMS for "SMS-based money management (apps that track and manage budget)", but only after review, only when no alternative exists, and with the condition that apps "may not exfiltrate or share non-financial or personal SMS history" ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10208820)). Approval is discretionary: Bluecoins was refused and dropped its SMS feature ([Bluecoins](https://bluecoinsapp.com/?p=9286)). Bank and UPI alerts also appear as notifications, so the notification listener is probably the more approvable primary route. That is an inference; no one researched Play's policy on notification listeners. Telegram's bot terms require a registered privacy policy, encryption at rest with keys stored separately, deletion on request, and **no training of AI models on user data** ([Telegram](https://telegram.org/tos/bot-developers)). Google's Workspace Limited Use policy bans using Gmail-derived data for ads, credit scoring or lending ([Google](https://developers.google.com/workspace/workspace-api-user-data-developer-policy)). That alone rules out mixing Gmail capture with loan monetisation.

## Five free-tier and policy risks need fixing before new features

The research identifies risks that are already live in Aaj's current build. Each has a free fix. The most serious is the parsing pipeline. Gemini's Unpaid Services terms say **"Do not submit sensitive, confidential, or personal information"**, allow Google to use inputs to improve its models, and let human reviewers read them ([Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)). Bank SMS and email bodies are exactly that kind of data. Free quotas are also no longer published and are "not guaranteed" ([Gemini rate limits](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits)). The fix is to parse on the device with templates first. Bank alerts are highly templated, and open-source Indian parsers exist, including `transaction-sms-parser`, a JS package that runs in React Native ([GitHub](https://github.com/saurabhgupta050890/transaction-sms-parser)). Use on-device Gemini Nano where ML Kit reports it as available, which is currently recent flagships only ([ML Kit GenAI](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/genai)). Send only the unmatched long tail to a server model, after masking account numbers, names and phone numbers. Masked bank text is arguably still personal data, so the safest free option for that long tail is a self-hosted open model, for example on Oracle's Always Free A1 instance ([Oracle](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/FreeTier/freetier_topic-Always_Free_Resources.htm)). The other option is to ask the user to confirm the transaction fields manually. Gemini's paid tier removes the terms problem, but it costs money. Groq's free voice tier (whisper-large-v3, 2,000 requests a day per organisation, [Groq](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits)) handles audio. Its data terms were not checked, so verify them before relying on it.

The EAS cap deserves a special note, because the project's working rule is "OTA updates first." Past 1,000 monthly users that rule stops working on the free plan, so the release process needs a store-build path before growth arrives. Two more limits are not urgent but worth knowing. Telegram caps free broadcasts at about **30 messages per second** and advises spreading notifications over hours ([Telegram](https://core.telegram.org/bots/faq)), so morning briefs should be staggered once the bot has thousands of users. Hosting the database in Supabase's Mumbai region is prudent even though RBI's payment-data localisation rule likely does not bind a tracker.

## Weekly, personal nudges and India's calendar drive retention

Finance apps lose almost everyone fast: day-30 retention is about **5% on Android and 9% on iOS** ([Liftoff](https://liftoff.ai/es/?p=35620%2F)). The strongest controlled evidence on what helps comes from a 1.93-million-customer bank megastudy published in 2025. **Weekly** reminders that congratulated people who saved and nudged those who didn't were the best of seven campaigns. Monthly reminders, salary-day timing and "save first" framing had no significant effect. Even the winner faded within **2–3 months** of stopping ([PNAS Nexus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12477529/)). Aaj's morning and evening briefs fit this pattern well. A weekly review keyed to each user's own behaviour should be added, and nudging should be permanent, with rotating content, rather than run as campaigns. Gamified goal tracking with progress bars and visible deadlines helped people reach about **20% more of their goal** in a UK RCT ([Bayes Business School](https://www.bayes.citystgeorges.ac.uk/news-and-events/news/2022/january/turning-long-term-savings-goals-into-a-game-can-increase-consumer-financial-well-being)). Streaks have no controlled finance evidence, so treat Aaj's existing streaks as an experiment and measure them against D30. Reminders can backfire too. In Brazil, credit-card due-date reminders cut late fees by 14% but raised overdraft fees by 9% ([SUFE summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/352133)). Every bill nudge should therefore show which account will pay and its balance.

The best-quantified feature is recurring-payment detection. In a 2022 US survey, people underestimated their subscription spend by **$133 a month**, and 42% had forgotten one they were still paying ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/09/06/consumers-underestimate-monthly-subscription-costs-by-at-least-100.html)). In India that means OTT, telecom and UPI AutoPay mandates, all visible in SMS and email Aaj already reads. Categorisation accuracy is what reviewers use to separate the leading US apps ([personal-finance-tools](https://personal-finance-tools.pages.dev/blog/copilot-money-ai-review-2026)), so per-user correction rules ("always tag this merchant as Food") pay off directly. Install-time backfill of 30–90 days of SMS and forwarded email produces a first insight on day one ("4 subscriptions, ₹1,847 a month"). Manual trackers can't match that, and their need for manual entry is widely blamed for abandonment.

India's calendar supplies built-in moments to re-engage users. Under the new **Income-tax Act 2025**, in force from 1 April 2026, the new-regime slabs are unchanged for FY 2026-27. Income up to **₹12 lakh** is tax-free through the rebate, and about ₹12.75 lakh for salaried people after the ₹75,000 standard deduction ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/tax/story/income-tax-slabs-fy-2026-27-will-rates-change-from-april-1-under-new-tax-rules-details-523303-2026-04-01)). Advance tax falls due on 15 June, 15 September, 15 December and 15 March ([ClearTax](https://www.cleartax.in/s/advance-tax)). ITR-1 and ITR-2 are due 31 July ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/tax/story/itr-filing-rules-revamped-from-april-1-deadlines-pan-itr-u-changes-explained-524440-2026-04-07)). A regime comparison calculator, reminders on those dates and a July filing checklist count as generic tax information, not advice. Festivals and weddings are the other big moments. Urban households expect to spend about **₹20,000** between September and November, and **83%** will dip into savings ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/urban-india-is-turning-to-savings-to-fund-its-festive-shopping)). The average wedding budget is **₹39.5 lakh**, and only about 15% of couples borrow ([Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/banking/loan/big-fat-weddings-cost-couples-their-hard-earned-savings-only-15-per-cent-use-loans-report)). That argues for "sinking funds": automatic monthly set-asides for Diwali, weddings, school fees and insurance premiums, prompted in August before the season starts. Family is where Aaj can beat INDmoney outright. One person can track a parent's or the household's money without forcing every member to sign up.

For "ask your money", the safe pattern is to let the model choose from fixed, typed tools (spend by category and period, period comparison, bills due, forecast to salary) while SQL or TypeScript computes every number and the model only phrases the reply. A semantic layer of predefined metrics produced reliable answers at M1 Finance ([dbt Coalesce 2024](https://www.getdbt.com/resources/coalesce-on-demand/coalesce-2024-building-a-hallucination-proof-sql-ai-with-metricflow-and-llms)). Showing the filters used ("Food, 1–29 Oct, 42 transactions") lets users check the answer. Monarch and Copilot both call their assistants "not an advisor" ([toolradar](https://toolradar.com/guides/best-ai-financial-assistants)), and Aaj should use the same framing for SEBI reasons.

## Monetise with low prices and no product pushing

The monetisation evidence points away from the model every Indian incumbent uses. Commission on loans and investments is both regulated and fatal to Aaj's main selling point. Gmail-derived data cannot legally feed lending decisions at all ([Google](https://developers.google.com/workspace/workspace-api-user-data-developer-policy)). Mint's end shows the trust cost of depending on referral revenue ([NBC](https://www.nbcmiami.com/news/business/money-report/budgeting-app-mint-is-shutting-down-to-the-disappointment-of-loyal-users-this-is-the-peril-of-relying-on-free-services/3153432/)). The sequence that fits the free-tools constraint starts with donations while Aaj runs on Vercel Hobby, because Vercel explicitly says donations do not count as commercial use ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/limits/fair-use-guidelines)). Whether Play requires Play Billing for an in-app tip button was not verified, so keep donation links on the web and Telegram for now. Once a paid tier makes sense, add a low-priced Pro plan or a one-time unlock for family, exports for a CA, and advanced reports. It has to be priced well below the roughly $100 a year Western apps charge, because Indian users get tracking free everywhere. Google Play takes **15%** on subscriptions, and India's user-choice billing is 4 points cheaper ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=266985)). GST registration is generally needed only past **₹20 lakh** in annual turnover, though sources conflict on OIDAR supplies from India, which a CA should resolve ([Bilimoria Mehta](https://www.bilimoriamehta.in/blogs/gst-registration-applicability-an-overview); [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/oidar-compliance-india-gst-registration-ntor-gstr5a-digital-tax-43951.html/)). Charging money turns the deployment commercial, so the API has to move to Cloudflare Workers or a self-hosted Oracle VM first. Cloudflare's free plan allows 100,000 requests a day but only 10 ms of CPU per request ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)). Its commercial-use terms were not checked. Price transparency matters as much as price: YNAB's hikes and silent renewals are its most persistent source of anger ([Pocketclear](https://pocketclear.app/blog/ynab-complaints-problems.html)).

Several numbers here are uncertain and should not be quoted to users. Gemini's real free quotas, the AA fee amount, BBPS and Setu pricing, Groq's data terms, and Indian willingness to pay for finance-app subscriptions are all unknown. No research covered Play's notification-listener policy or the demand for vernacular expense trackers in particular.

## Conclusion

Aaj's lack of a licence is less of a handicap than it looks. The licensed pipes (AA, bureaus, BBPS) mostly benefit companies whose business is selling credit or investments, and they cost money with every fetch. A tracker built on what users forward, upload and receive on their own phones reaches nearly the same coverage, minus live balances, while being able to say honestly that it sells nothing and needs no one's permission to exist. That makes the order of work clear. Legal and data-safety hygiene comes first, because the riskiest parts of today's build (bank text on a free LLM tier, Gmail OAuth, no backups) are the parts users trust Aaj with most. Each later gate then converts into money or paperwork only when revenue justifies it.

The less obvious implication concerns where the product's power comes from. Google Pay's free multilingual assistant shows that chat itself is now a commodity. Aaj's advantage is the set of verified numbers behind the chat: every bank, cash, the family and the household calendar of festivals, weddings and tax dates. A small, deterministic toolset over that data, delivered as a weekly personal nudge, is both the most defensible feature and the one with the best evidence for retention.
