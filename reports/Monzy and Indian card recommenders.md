# Real spending data beats Monzy's six questions

Monzy is a free, web-only quiz from personal-finance creator Ankur Warikoo, launched in May 2026. It asks about six multiple-choice questions and returns one "perfect match" credit card. It has no account system, no eligibility check, no affiliate links and, so far, no visible revenue. Its ranking matches the user's stated preferences against card attributes. It does not calculate the rupee value of rewards from what the user actually spends. That gap is exactly where Aaj is strong. Aaj already knows each user's real monthly spending by category, merchant and card, so it can calculate what every card would have earned and rank cards by the gain over the user's current best card. Monzy's quiz, which buckets spending into three bands, cannot do that, and in India no app does it at scale. The rest of the market runs on commissions. Paisabazaar, BankBazaar, the CashKaro group (BankKaro, EarnKaro, Great.Cards) and CardInsider earn per issued card or from co-branded cards, and users complain about spam calls and fake "pre-approved" offers. The answer is yes: Aaj should build it. Build it in this order: first a "missed rewards" report on cards the user already holds, then a top-3 "next card" ranking with plain bank links, and add commission links only later, if at all. The money is small. Affiliate payouts are about ₹1,500 per activated card, which works out to roughly ₹6,000–18,000 a year per 1,000 monthly users. From **1 January 2027**, RBI's new marketing rules treat any paid referrer as the bank's DSA/DMA (direct selling or direct marketing agent), and most DPDP data-protection duties start on **13 May 2027**. The product's value is retention and trust. Commission income is a small extra that also binds Aaj to bank conduct rules.

## Monzy is Warikoo's free six-question funnel, not a spend engine

The link the user followed, `monzy.co/go/ytn`, is a 302 redirect to the homepage tagged `utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=description&utm_campaign=ytlatest`. In other words it is the link Warikoo puts in his YouTube video descriptions, and "ytn" is a campaign code ([monzy.co/go/ytn](https://monzy.co/go/ytn)). The pitch is "60 seconds · 1 recommendation · NOT sponsored by any banks". It attacks rival sites for "Endless Lists", "Biased Rankings", "Data Sold" and "Spam Calls" ([monzy.co/credit-cards](https://monzy.co/credit-cards)). The privacy policy keeps that promise: "You do not need an account. We do not ask for your name, email, phone, or PAN." Monzy stores anonymous quiz answers, Google Analytics and Microsoft Clarity data, and counts of apply-link clicks ([privacy policy](https://monzy.co/privacy-policy)). The terms call Monzy "not a bank, not a lender, and not a financial advisor". They prohibit scraping and name no legal entity or grievance officer ([terms](https://monzy.co/terms-and-conditions)). The site's structured data gives the founding date as **6 May 2026** and the founder as Warikoo ([monzy.co/about](https://monzy.co/about)). His LinkedIn headline claims a "16M+ community" ([LinkedIn](https://in.linkedin.com/in/warikoo)), and his own site calls Monzy "a free credit card discovery platform" with "No ads, no sponsorships" ([ankurwarikoo.com](https://ankurwarikoo.com/about/)). The team is five people. A data lead who has tracked Indian cards since 2019 built the card database, and a chief developer built the engine ([team page](https://blog.monzy.co/blog/team-monzy)).

The quiz was walked once on 1 November 2026, through the Shopping branch, using anonymous answers only. It runs as follows. The user picks a category (Travel, Shopping, or Bills & Utility, each with three sub-preferences). Then come annual-fee tolerance in five bands, where most online spending goes (Amazon, Flipkart, Myntra, food delivery, Tata apps, OTT, mixed), whether UPI support is needed, total monthly spending in three bands (under ₹15,000, ₹15,000–50,000, ₹50,000+), and finally capped-but-higher versus uncapped-but-lower rewards. The progress bar jumped from 37% to 59%, so the questions branch. The quiz never asks for income, credit score, date of birth or contact details. The server picks each next question through `/api/start` and `/api/answer`, behind reCAPTCHA v3 ([monzy.co/credit-cards](https://monzy.co/credit-cards)). Monzy's methodology page describes three steps. Step 1 is a "simulated preference exercise" that weights the attributes the user cares about. Step 2 applies hard filters: lifetime-free versus fee, cashback versus points, UPI or not. Step 3 is a "validate the fit" pass that penalises cards whose headline rate is undermined by exclusions. Scores are per persona, such as "7.1 / 10 for online shoppers" ([scoring methodology](https://monzy.co/scoring-methodology)). The card data behind it is good. Each card carries category earn rates with monthly rupee caps, caveats and the bank's source URL, and it already reflects April 2026 changes such as the SBI Cashback ₹2,000 sub-cap ([refined catalogue](https://monzy.co/api/refined-catalog)). Monzy quotes three different catalogue sizes: 348 cards in the UI, 335 from the API, and "493 cards" in its structured data. That points to discontinued variants, or to inflated marketing.

The test exposed the method's limits. An Amazon shopper with a sub-₹500 fee tolerance and ₹15,000–50,000 of monthly spending was matched to the **CheQ AU LED card**. It is a niche card marked "No CIBIL Check Needed", and its 12% headline rate works only when spending runs through the CheQ app ([monzy.co/credit-cards](https://monzy.co/credit-cards)). A quiz cannot tell whether a user's real Amazon spend is ₹2,000 or ₹20,000 a month. So it cannot tell whether a capped 12% beats a simpler uncapped 5%, or whether the user already holds a card that earns most of the value. After the result, "Visit Card Site" goes straight to the bank's product page with only `?utm_source=monzy` added. There is no lead form, no affiliate network and no DSA flow ([card-apply-urls](https://monzy.co/api/card-apply-urls)). The `/go/` redirect layer would make it easy to switch to commission links later, but nothing shows that is planned. Traction is thin and unaudited. Monzy's Product Hunt launch drew 8 upvotes and finished #33 for the day ([hunted.space](https://hunted.space/product/monzy)). No funding, entity registration, user counts or app listing were found. Searches of Reddit and Product Hunt found no independent reviews or complaints ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/monzy)). That may reflect search-index limits rather than real absence. Monzy's moat is distribution through Warikoo's audience, not the engine. It has no expense tracking, card linking or reward tracking. Its calculators (a reward calculator, a fee breakeven calculator, a "card stack checker") expect users to type in their own numbers.

## India's card-advice market runs on per-card commissions

Four business models dominate, and only one of them pays for advice that is purely on the user's side. The large marketplaces earn per card issued and draw users in with free credit scores. PB Fintech's Paisabazaar issued **3.5 lakh cards in FY26** ([Quartr](https://quartr.com/events/pb-fintech-policybzr-q4-25-26_FeB3abM1)). Its own guide admits that "pre-approved doesn't mean guaranteed approval" and that applying triggers a hard enquiry ([Paisabazaar](https://www.paisabazaar.com/credit-score/do-pre-approved-credit-card-offers-affect-your-credit-score-aio/)). BankBazaar booked FY25 operating revenue of about ₹249–254 crore. The CEO credits about **₹210 crore of recurring revenue to co-branded card distribution**, roughly 84% of the total ([Newsdrum](https://www.newsdrum.in/business/bankbazaar-topline-crosses-rs-250-cr-earns-rs-4-cr-profit-10601288)). Its "comparison" therefore has a strong reason to favour its own cards. The CashKaro group had about ₹350 crore of revenue in FY25, with finance products around 20% of it ([Indian Startup News](https://indianstartupnews.com/news/cashback-platform-cashkaro-revenue-jumps-to-rs-350-crore-in-fy25-9652039)). Through EarnKaro it pays sub-affiliates "up to ₹1,750" per SBI card ([EarnKaro](https://earnkaro.com/blog/idfc-bank-affiliate-program/)). Its Great.Cards front end ("Find My Ultimate Card", "Beat My Card") offers gift cards worth up to ₹3,000 "tracked on the CashKaro Wallet", with no visible disclosure that it earns commission ([great.cards](https://great.cards/)). A third group is fintechs with their own co-branded cards: CRED (the IndusInd RuPay card, launched in September 2025), Scapia (Federal Bank) and Kiwi (RuPay on UPI). They compete for the "which card" decision but are not neutral ([IndusInd](https://www.indusind.bank.in/iblogs/pressrelease/launch-of-cred-indusind-bank-rupay-credit-card/)). CRED Money analyses Account Aggregator data, but for money management, not for recommending cards ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/24/cred-launches-personal-finance-manager-for-indias-affluent/)).

The fourth group, subscription reward optimisers, comes closest to what Aaj could build. SaveSage has raised about $1.7M and claims "7.5 Lakh+" users and spending analysis "across 1L+ data points". It does not say how it collects spending data ([savesage.club](https://www.savesage.club/); [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/snippets/savesage-raises-1-mn-in-pre-seed-round-10604410)). PointsFly launched publicly on 16 August 2026 with an AI agent that picks the best card for each purchase, priced at **₹249 a month or ₹2,999 a year**. It reported 250+ paying users in its first week ([Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/pointsfly/pricing); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/companies/pointsfly)). Credeem, ccreward and Cusp are smaller apps on the same idea ([Credeem](https://www.producthunt.com/p/credeem-credit-cards-rewards-for-india/credeem-launching-v2); [ccreward](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/ccreward/id6736835206)). The US equivalents prove that people will pay. CardPointers+ costs about $60–72 a year and MaxRewards Gold about $84 a year ([College Investor](https://thecollegeinvestor.com/46251/kudos-review/)). One App Store review notes that CardPointers does not support Indian cards ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/id1472875808?l=hi)).

Users complain about the same things across the category. An IANS report from August 2025 described "a flood of spam calls" after CIBIL checks on Bajaj Finance and Paisabazaar ([IANS](https://ianslive.in/cibils-role-under-scanner-users-complain-of-spam-calls-after-credit-checks-on-bajaj-finance--20250822150135)). TechnoFino threads are titled "Please don't apply through Paisabazaar and BankBazaar for CC" and warn that only the card issuer can truly pre-approve ([TechnoFino](https://technofino.in/community/threads/please-dont-apply-through-paisabazzar-and-bankbazaar-for-cc.1745/latest); [TechnoFino](https://technofino.in/community/goto/post?id=52451)). Disclosure is rare. CardAdvisor's "how we make money" page is an exception, not the norm ([CardAdvisor](https://cardadvisor.in/how-we-make-money)). Bias towards high payouts follows from the incentives, but no source proves that any specific site orders its rankings by payout. Card data goes stale fast. Rule changes cluster in April and July: Axis moved Flipkart card dining from MCC (merchant category code) to Axis's own categories in June 2025, SBI Cashback got ₹2,000 online and offline sub-caps in April 2026, and HDFC Infinia got a 3,000-point monthly voucher sub-cap in July 2026 ([CardTrail](https://www.cardtrail.in/rewards/reward-devaluation-hdfc-axis-icici-2025/); [CardAdvisor tracker](https://cardadvisor.in/articles/credit-card-devaluation-tracker-india-2025-2026)). One vendor claims that as of early 2026 "no mainstream Indian app offers real-time 'use this card here' recommendations" ([cardtrail](https://cardtrail.pages.dev/calculators/credit-card-spend-category-optimizer-india)). That is still true if "mainstream" means at scale. No Indian player was found that combines parsing of real transactions with both "which card to get" and "which card to use now". This research also left a long list of players unchecked, among them CardExpert, TechnoFino's card finder, INDmoney, Jupiter and axio/Walnut.

## From 1 January 2027 a paid referrer counts as the bank's agent

The card rules were rewritten in late 2025. The **RBI (Commercial Banks – Credit Cards and Debit Cards: Issuance and Conduct) Directions, 2025** took effect on 28 November 2025. Under them the card issuer alone decides whether to issue a card (para 11(10)), unsolicited cards are "strictly prohibited", and consent "shall be explicit and shall not be implied". The issuer "shall also be liable for the acts of their agents" (para 44) and must give agents only the customer data they need ([TaxGuru reproduction](https://taxguru.in/rbi/reserve-bank-india-commercial-banks-credit-cards-debit-cards-issuance-conduct-directions-2025.html)). The bigger change comes next. RBI's advertising, marketing and sale amendments were issued on 15 June 2026 and take effect on **1 January 2027**. They define a DSA/DMA as anyone other than a bank employee whom the bank engages to "sell, market, promote or influence" customers, "irrespective of the contractual designation or nomenclature used". Banks must publish their agent lists and get each agent to sign a code of conduct. The amendments require explicit consent with "No" as the default, ban unsolicited push notifications, list eleven banned dark patterns (including false urgency, disguised advertising and nagging), and require suitability checks. Where mis-selling is established, the customer gets a full refund plus compensation ([CorpLawUpdates](https://www.corplawupdates.in/updates/rbi-tightens-rules-advertising-marketing-sale-financial-products-regulated-entities); [RBI circular index](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_CircularIndexDisplay.aspx?Id=13485)). This research reads the definition as very likely covering a commission-earning recommender. Whether a sub-affiliate under EarnKaro or Cuelinks counts as a "sub-agent" is a question for a lawyer. Either way, expect banks and networks to push codes of conduct, audit rights and indemnities down to the referrer. A recommender that earns nothing, as Monzy does today, sits outside the "engaged by a bank" test.

The data rails stay closed to a solo developer, which shapes how eligibility can work. Becoming a credit bureau "Specified User" needs net worth of at least ₹2 crore, three years of experience processing data for credit institutions, and diversified ownership. Bureau data cannot be passed on to banks ([TaxGuru CIC Directions](https://taxguru.in/rbi/rbi-credit-information-companies-directions-2025.html)). Each bureau must give one free full report a year. Account Aggregator data goes only to entities regulated by RBI, SEBI, IRDAI or PFRDA ([Setu docs](https://docs.setu.co/data/account-aggregator/licenses-and-go-live/participants-in-aa)). The Digital Lending Directions, 2025 cover loan service providers. Lawyers disagree on whether credit cards fall under them ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/05/14/rbi-digital-lending-directions-legal-news/)). They clearly cover personal loans and BNPL (buy now, pay later), so Aaj should stay out of loans and EMI offers. Google Play allows READ_SMS for "SMS-based money management" only as core functionality, and lists "any transfer that results in a sale of this data" as invalid use ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10208820?hl=en)). The safe pattern is to compute recommendations from Aaj's own parsed data and send a partner nothing but a click. The DPDP Rules were notified on 13 November 2025, and most fiduciary duties and penalties (up to ₹250 crore for security failures) start on **13 May 2027** ([ConsentOS](https://consentos.in/learn/dpdp-compliance-timeline/); [Digimart](https://www.digimartinfotech.in/dpdp-rules-2025-deadlines-penalties-guide.php)). Using transaction data to recommend cards is a new purpose. It should get its own consent, separate from expense tracking.

The advertising and tax rules are lighter but real. ASCI's April 2025 finance-influencer addendum accepts labels such as "ad", "sponsored" and "affiliate", and requires SEBI registration only for investment advice ([Exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/advertising-news/asci-updates-influencer-guidelines-with-respect-to-health-finance-influencers-142977.html)). A CCPA advisory from June 2025 asked platforms to audit themselves for dark patterns ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/ccpa-dark-patterns-warning-advisory-goes-out-to-streamers-fintechs-in-addition-to-e-commerce-firms-69331.htm)). Referral commission attracts 18% GST, and TDS under s.194H is 2% above ₹20,000 a year ([TaxBuddy](https://www.taxbuddy.com/blog/section-194h-tds-rate-in-2025-detailed-explanation); [Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/learn/gst-on-brokerage-and-commission/)). Whether commission agents must register for GST below the ₹20 lakh turnover threshold is disputed ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=686425)), so a CA should confirm. One limit from Aaj's own stack matters most right now. Vercel's Hobby plan bans commercial use, and affiliate links count, so the API has to move to a free host that allows commercial use (Cloudflare Workers or Oracle's Always Free tier) before any commission link goes live ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/limits/fair-use-guidelines)). No RBI or CCPA action against a card-comparison aggregator was found. RBI does fine card issuers regularly, for example American Express in October 2025 ([RBI](https://website.rbi.org.in/web/rbi/-/press-releases/rbi-imposes-monetary-penalty-on-american-express-banking-corp)), and from 2027 banks will pass the cost of mis-selling refunds on to whoever referred the customer.

## Aaj should build a missed-rewards engine first and a card picker second

The case for building rests on one asymmetry. Monzy asks a user to guess their spending in three bands. Aaj already measures it, by merchant and by card. The first feature should be a monthly **"you left ₹X on the table"** report, which needs no new card and no commission. It answers: "Your ₹4,200 on Swiggy went on card A at 1%. Card B, which you already hold, pays 5%." This hook works on India's roughly **124 million existing cards**, while net additions run at only about 0.9 million a month ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/economy/credit-card-spending-declines-by-2-8-to-2-02-trillion-in-august-2026); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/banking/story/credit-card-spends-jump-to-rs219-lakh-crore-in-march-hdfc-hits-record-share-sbi-outperforms-527779-2026-04-28)). The second feature ranks the top three cards the user does not hold by the **gain over the user's current best setup**, not by how good each card is on its own. A Telegram `/bestcard` command and a "use this card" hint before recurring spends (Amazon, fuel, rent, food delivery) can come later.

The formula is simple to write down and expensive to keep accurate. For each candidate card, over the last 3–12 months of categorised spending, annualised:

`Net value = Σ_categories min(spend_c × rate_c × point_value, cap_c) + milestone bonuses reached − excluded spend × rate (rent, wallet, fuel, insurance, gold, government) + lounge visits used × ₹1,000–1,500 − forex markup × foreign spend − (annual fee × 1.18 unless spending clears the waiver threshold)`

`Score = Net value(card) − Net value(user's current best card for each category)`

Point value needs a table per card and per redemption route (cashback, vouchers, air miles), because devaluations often hit redemption rather than earn rates. Infinia's voucher cap and the Atlas transfer-partner changes in 2026 are examples ([CardAdvisor tracker](https://cardadvisor.in/articles/credit-card-devaluation-tracker-india-2025-2026)). SMS carries merchant names but not MCCs, and Axis now uses its own internal categories. So merchant-to-category mapping has to be curated and tagged with a confidence level. UPI-on-RuPay spends should be detected separately, because many cards exclude UPI or reward it less. In Aaj's free stack this becomes a Supabase `cards` table, with rules stored as JSON plus a `verified_at` date and a source URL. A server-side function maps Aaj's spending categories onto card categories. The MVP should cover **30–40 mass-market cards**, built by hand from bank T&C pages. Monzy's catalogue is not an option: its terms forbid scraping. A monthly review should run against free trackers (CardAdvisor, CardTrail, InvestingPro), with an extra review in each April and July window, and the UI should show "rules verified on [date]". A GitHub Actions cron can flag cards that have not been verified in more than 45 days.

Eligibility should be estimated, never promised. Card issuers publish income thresholds, not score cut-offs. HDFC Regalia Gold needs gross monthly income above ₹1.5 lakh for salaried applicants ([HDFC](https://www.hdfc.bank.in/credit-cards/regalia-gold-credit-card/eligibility)). Axis Magnus needs ₹24 lakh a year ([InvestingPro](https://www.investingpro.in/articles/axis-magnus-credit-card-eligibility-2026)). SBI SimplyCLICK lists a 700+ score and ₹20,000 a month ([EarnKaro SBI](https://earnkaro.com/blog/sbi-affiliate-program)). Aaj can sort cards into "likely", "stretch" and "unlikely" bands. The inputs are the salary credit detected in SMS, the cards and limits the user already holds, the banks the user already uses (existing customers get approved more easily), and a score band the user enters or reads from a free bureau report. The word "pre-approved" should never appear. The guardrails matter more than the formula. Hide every "apply" button when SMS shows minimum-due-only payments, late fees, high utilisation or EMI bounces, and show a debt-first message instead. Outstanding card balances were about ₹3.1 lakh crore in March 2026 ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/economy/credit-card-spending-declines-by-2-8-to-2-02-trillion-in-august-2026)). Rank on user value alone, and say so on screen. Send a bank or network only a plain deep link, never a name, phone number or spending profile. Make card suggestions opt-in, with "No" as the default. Send no promotional push notifications without that opt-in. Gate users at 18+, and keep loans, EMI offers and insurance out entirely.

## Commission income stays in the low lakhs until Aaj reaches tens of thousands of users

Commission income is real but small, and slow to arrive. EarnKaro's rate card lists SBI cards at a flat ₹2,240, Axis co-brands at ₹2,380, HDFC co-brands at ₹1,500, ICICI at up to ₹2,400 and Axis Magnus or Atlas at up to ₹2,975. Most of these pay on activation and are confirmed within 75 days ([EarnKaro](https://earnkaro.com/blog/credit-card-affiliate-programs)). Cuelinks warns that validation can take 60–90 days and that banks' own refer-and-earn schemes pay only about ₹250–500 ([Cuelinks](https://www.cuelinks.com/blog/best-credit-card-affiliate-programs/)). Joining these networks is free, but the user has to sign up personally. No published Indian figures exist for conversion from monthly users to approved cards. The figures below are therefore planning assumptions from this research, not measured data: 30% of users view a recommendation, 5–10% of viewers click apply, 25–40% of applications are approved and activated, and the blended payout is ₹1,500. Aaj's real spending data could push conversion above quiz-tool levels, but no source shows by how much.

Subscriptions are the steadier alternative. PointsFly charges ₹249 a month, and CardPointers and MaxRewards charge $60–84 a year in the US. If 1–2% of 10,000 users paid ₹49–99 a month for "use this card" alerts, devaluation alerts and the full missed-rewards history, Aaj would earn about ₹0.6–2.4 lakh a year. Play Billing takes 15% of that. Indian willingness to pay for this is unproven. A third option avoids conflicts entirely: keep everything free and donation-funded, which Vercel Hobby permits. Aaj's earlier roadmap advised against card referrals because they weaken its "we never sell you a loan" promise. Commission-free recommendations keep that promise, so the sequencing below keeps commission links optional and late.

The main risks follow from the incentives. A bad pick caused by stale rules damages trust faster than any revenue repays it, because the user can see their own numbers. Commission links bring bank conduct rules, mis-selling refund exposure passed down by contract, and Play's ban on "sale" of SMS-derived data if any profile ever leaves the device or server. Monzy could also add a spending upload or commission links at any time, and Warikoo's reach dwarfs Aaj's. Aaj's defence is data it already holds, not marketing.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. Building a card picker is not the hard part. Monzy shows a five-person team can ship a credible one in weeks, and Monzy's best asset is a hand-maintained rules database, not an algorithm. What Aaj owns, and Monzy and the commission-funded incumbents do not, is the measured counterfactual: what the user's own spending would have earned on each card. That makes the most valuable output an existing-card optimiser that pays off every month, rather than a one-off "apply now" funnel. It also suits the market, because India's card base grows slowly and is crowded with devaluation-weary holders.

The 2027 rules turn commission income from a free extra into a choice about Aaj's identity. Before January 2027, a commission link is a small affiliate tag. After it, the link is most likely an agent relationship with codes of conduct, refund liability and consent audits, in exchange for a few lakh rupees a year even at 50,000 users. The better course is to build the spend-based engine now with no commission, measure how many users act on it, and decide in 2027 whether that proven trust is worth more as subscriptions or as commission links. Several things are still unknown. Monzy's real usage, Indian conversion and willingness-to-pay rates, how the spend-based competitors collect their data, and the exact primary RBI text all remain unverified. The DSA/sub-agent and GST questions need a lawyer and a CA before any commission income starts.
